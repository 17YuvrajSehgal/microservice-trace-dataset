\title{Context Is a Pipeline, Not a Window:\\
Information Attrition in LLM Agent Harnesses}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Submitted for double-anonymous review}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Long-context LLM agents are evaluated on capacity and final accuracy. Those
measures miss what is lost \emph{before} the model receives a tool result. In
a tool-using root-cause-analysis agent evaluated against injected faults, we
found the context window used to a median of $6.6\%$ while $3{,}827$ of
$11{,}189$ tool results were truncated in transit --- every one into invalid
JSON. The loss was positional, not random: the harness bounded results by
slicing serialized JSON, which deletes late keys outright, so one field
reached the model in $4.7\%$ of calls while its sibling, in the same payloads,
reached it $99.3\%$ of the time. Which field carries a diagnostic culprit
depends on whether the culprit process started during the trace, and the
benchmark's accuracy split lined up exactly with that delivery asymmetry ---
motivating a controlled test of whether delivery loss \emph{caused} it. We
contribute a pipeline model of agent context with an \emph{attrition profile}
that measures loss at each stage; replay evidence over $864$ recorded tool
results, including a production-scale trace where the deployed bound deletes a
resident culprit's entire evidence class ($0$ of $128$ records); a controlled
ablation showing the defect's cost appears as \emph{work} before error ---
$+28\%$ tool calls, computations roughly doubled, evidence yield falling
$0.61\rightarrow0.31$ --- while accuracy survives wherever redundant evidence
paths exist; and a survey showing positional bounding is the norm in current
agent frameworks. Whether delivery loss causes the accuracy split remains
unresolved, and we report the experiments that failed to settle it.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
LLM agents, agent harnesses, context engineering, empirical evaluation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An LLM agent that uses tools does not read its context window; it reads
whatever its harness decided to put there. Between a tool producing a result
and a model conditioning on it sit transforms an engineer wrote: the result is
serialized, bounded to a budget, retained or trimmed across turns, handed
between agent phases, and discarded at the end of the run. Each is a design
decision. Each is lossy.

Research on agent context concentrates on capacity and on attention within the
window~\cite{liu2024lost}, or on memory architectures that extend
it~\cite{packer2023memgpt}. Harness papers treat scaffolding as an engineering
detail~\cite{yang2024sweagent,jimenez2024swebench}; a recent source-code
anatomy of eleven production harnesses catalogues the subsystems without
measuring the effect of any design choice~\cite{harness2026}; and failure
taxonomies classify what went wrong across thousands of agent
trajectories~\cite{cemri2025mast}. That last line of work is the closest to
ours, and the gap between them is the point: \textbf{a trajectory records the
result a tool \emph{delivered}, not the one it \emph{produced}}, so a loss on
the way into the transcript cannot be recovered from the transcript. No
current evaluation reports how much of what an agent produced survived to its
decision.

This paper reports what we found when we measured that. In a kernel-trace
root-cause-analysis agent evaluated against labelled fault injections, the
context window was nearly empty --- the largest single prompt in $120$ runs
was $23$k tokens against a $400$k window, and the median run peaked at
$6.6\%$ --- while a third of all tool results were destroyed in transit.
Because the serializer preserves key order, the harness's prefix-slice bound
did not \emph{sample} oversized results; it \emph{deleted late keys outright}.
One list of containers reached the model in $4.7\%$ of $742$ calls; its
sibling list, in the same payloads, in $99.3\%$.

The consequence lined up with task structure. A process injected as a fault
\emph{spawns} mid-trace and lands in the early, surviving list; a container
that was throttled or degraded was \emph{already running} and appears only in
the late, deleted one. Localisation accuracy split along exactly that line on
both subject applications: spawn-type faults $40$--$55$ of $60$,
already-running faults $0$--$24$ of $60$. We had read those numbers as a
property of kernel traces. The delivery measurements say the harness
contributed --- how much is precisely what our controlled experiments set out
to establish, and we report below both what they established and what they
could not.

We contribute: (1) a model of agent context as a five-stage pipeline whose
stages fail in characteristic, measurable ways (Sec.~\ref{sec:pipeline});
(2) the \emph{attrition profile} --- per-stage loss measurements and an
\emph{evidence-yield} summary --- applied to our own harness
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}); (3) mechanism and replay evidence, including the
deployed bound deleting a culprit's entire evidence class on a
production-scale trace (Sec.~\ref{sec:measured}); (4) a registered,
controlled ablation whose headline is a \emph{compensation tax}: the defect
leaves accuracy intact wherever redundant evidence paths exist, and converts
the loss into extra work (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}); and (5) a source survey
showing positional bounding is the norm in today's agent frameworks
(Sec.~\ref{sec:measured}).

\section{Context as a Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

We model the path from tool output to decision as five boundaries
(Table~\ref{tab:pipeline}). The stages are not optional: any tool-using agent
must serialize results, bound them, keep or trim them across turns, and move
conclusions between phases; a harness that skips a stage has chosen a default
for it. And loss at a boundary is \emph{structured}, not stochastic: a
truncation rule interacts with the shape of the data. If results are
serialized with stable key order and bounded by a prefix, a field's survival
probability is a function of its position --- and nothing makes late fields
less important. The model layer is known to attend unevenly by
position~\cite{liu2024lost}; the harness layer adds a harder version of the
same axis, because a field past the cut is not attended to less --- it is gone
before attention sees it.

\textbf{The attrition profile.} We propose instrumenting these boundaries and
reporting, next to accuracy, what fraction of produced evidence survived each
one. Units must be defined per stage: at S1 the unit is a \emph{top-level
field of a serialized tool result}, counted as delivered when its content is
textually present in what the model receives (parseability is reported
separately, as its own harm); at S4 the unit is a \emph{recorded finding or
captured computation}, counted as transferred when it reaches the phase that
decides. Our summary statistic, \emph{evidence yield}, is the ratio of
findings recorded to computations produced within a run --- a deliberately
narrow, fully measurable proxy for ``work that reached the decision.'' We do
not claim it captures relevance; a relevance-weighted yield needs ground truth
and is future work.

\section{The Defect Class, Measured}
\label{sec:measured}

\subsection{Field evidence}
The numbers in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} come from the harness's deployed
evaluation: $720$ runs, $11{,}189$ tool results, $3{,}827$ truncated, all
unparseable; the $4.7\%$/$99.3\%$ split measured over $742$ enumeration calls.
The archival accuracy figures were produced by the strict scorer --- a claimed
container is verified against ground-truth namespace identity --- not by the
lenient scorer we later caught (and fixed) in our own ablation tooling.

\subsection{Mechanism, replay, and the deployed bound}
\label{sec:e1}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_cliff.pdf}
\caption{The delivery cliff. A constructed result of the real tool's shape is
replayed through both bounding implementations (no model involved), varying
only how many short-lived processes the trace contains. Under a prefix slice
the late field is delivered completely until the result crosses the bound,
then disappears and takes the payload's validity with it; a row-aware bound
keeps every field at every size.}
\label{fig:cliff}
\end{figure}

The mechanism is deterministic (Fig.~\ref{fig:cliff}). On real data: replaying
$864$ recorded tool results from $31$ runs through both regimes, $100\%$ of
results exceeding the bound became unparseable under the prefix slice and
$0\%$ under row-dropping. The tools that overflow are those that
\emph{enumerate} ($50\%$ of process-enumeration calls, $43\%$ of agent-written
analyses, $1\%$ of scalar summaries) --- and only $2.5\%$ of all calls were
affected, which is precisely why such a defect survives inspection while
removing the answer on the calls that matter.

The same replay at production scale: on a full $21$-container microservice
trace with a CPU-throttled service --- a \emph{resident, quiet} culprit, one
JVM among five at ${\sim}1.4\%$ of kernel events each --- the enumeration
result is $33{,}370$ characters, $5.6\times$ the deployed bound, no
calibration involved. The deployed slice delivers $30$ of $118$ early-field
records and \textbf{$0$ of $128$} late-field records, in an invalid payload.
At the deployed bound, on deployed data, a resident culprit's entire evidence
class vanishes before the model sees it.

\subsection{The pattern in the wild}
We read the tool-output bounding code of seven current agent-framework
releases (langchain-core 1.6.12, langchain 1.4.6, smolagents 1.26.0,
openai-agents 0.24.0, autogen-agentchat 0.7.5, pyautogen 0.10.0, crewai
0.11.2). Both general-purpose bounders we found are positional:
langchain-core applies a bare $500$-character prefix slice to serialized tool
outputs in its message-serialization path; smolagents deletes the
\emph{middle} of observations above $20$k characters. Neither inspects the
structure of what it cuts. Three frameworks bound nothing, deferring the loss
to the window; one truncates only tracing data. The single structure-aware
bound we found --- a shell middleware truncating by lines with an explicit
flag --- shows the repaired pattern is available; it is just not the default.

\section{Controlled Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

Field evidence is correlational, and the deployed repair shipped alongside a
capability change, so it cannot be attributed. We therefore reintroduced each
defect in isolation, with predictions registered before each wave of runs and
every deviation logged in a dated amendment file that ships with the artifact.

\textbf{Design.} Subjects are kernel traces of a coding agent under cgroup
resource limits with exact ground truth; the diagnosing agent, model
(temperature $0$), tools, and task are held fixed. Fault classes:
\emph{spawned} culprit (launched mid-trace) and \emph{resident} culprit
(running before tracing began), plus healthy negative controls; every cell
receives the same symptom-level operator hint. Configurations: \emph{repaired}
(row-aware bound, automatic phase transfer), \emph{S1 defect} (prefix slice at
the bound that binds on these traces), \emph{S1 reversed} (defect plus
reversed key order --- content identical, only field positions move), an
\emph{order-only} inertness control, \emph{S2/S3/S4} single-defect arms, and
\emph{all-defect}. The unit of analysis is $5$ distinct recordings per class
$\times$ $3$ repeats, paired across configurations; $145$ runs total, none
failing. Scoring requires a \emph{commitment} to a culprit in the answer
field, verified against ground truth; a mention in prose does not count ---
our first scorer credited mentions, inflating one arm from $2/15$ to $14/15$,
and was caught by auditing at $46$ cells and replaced.

\textbf{Results} (Table~\ref{tab:compensation}). Context capacity was never
the constraint (H1 held): cumulative input was a median $567$k tokens per run
across a median $53$ calls --- roughly $10$k per call against a $400$k window.
Under the defect the agent suffered a median $27$ destroyed, unparseable
results per run ($40\%$ of its calls) and localised the spawned culprit in
$15/15$ runs against $14/15$ repaired --- a difference within noise at this
sample size, not evidence the defect helps. What moved was the work: tool
calls $53\to68\to76$ ($+28\%$, $+43\%$), computations $18\to32\to36$ (roughly
doubling), input tokens $+5\%$ and $+14\%$, and evidence yield falling
$0.61\to0.34\to0.31$ --- the yield drop is inflation of wasted production, not
lost conclusions, and the order-only control restores exactly the baseline
$0.61$. At equal answers, those extra tokens are pure waste --- an energy cost
invisible to latency metrics.

\textbf{The reversal test, and an honest refutation.} We registered H2$'$:
reversing key order moves the culprit's list from fully delivered to fully
deleted, so accuracy should fall. Transcripts confirm the manipulation worked
--- the culprit was absent from every enumeration payload --- yet the agent
localised it $15/15$, recovering it through self-written aggregations, because
this culprit is the \emph{loudest process in the trace} ($14.0\%$ of kernel
events): every sorted result surfaces it inside the surviving prefix. The
order-only control was flat, so the recovery is real. What replaces H2$'$ is a
sharper conditional: \textbf{the harm of positional delivery loss is gated by
evidence-path redundancy, and its cost appears as work before it appears as
error.} A dominant signal survives severing any single path at a measured
compensation tax; a quiet culprit carried by one field --- the archival
resident case, delivered at $4.7\%$ --- has no second path.

\textbf{H3 and H4 could not be evaluated on this substrate.} The resident
class is floored \emph{when repaired} ($2/15$ commitments; the agent's cgroup
fault manifests as absence in a single-namespace trace), so there is no
false-negative headroom for H3's shift and no within-class accuracy variance
for H4's predictor comparison. Both remain registered, unevaluated hypotheses;
the S4 motivation stands on the field data (a worker that computed the
deciding rate --- $1{,}242.8$/s falling to $0$ --- and transferred nothing,
while the run concluded ``normal'').

\textbf{Three failed attempts at the causal accuracy link.} On the production
trace where the deployed bound deletes the resident culprit's evidence class
(Sec.~\ref{sec:e1}), the \emph{repaired} agent --- plain and
procedure-supplied --- also failed to localise the throttled service, twice
committing to the database one hop behind it. With no working baseline there
is nothing for the defect to destroy, so we did not run that matrix, and the
accuracy half of the archival split remains correlational: plausibly
capability and delivery \emph{jointly}. The delivery half is demonstrated; the
causal accuracy experiment, on a fault family with measured repaired-arm
headroom, is the first item of future work.

\section{Why This Goes Unnoticed}
\label{sec:unnoticed}

In kernel traces of two production coding agents ($n{=}10$ uncapped runs,
median; range $80$--$91\%$; includes network and queueing), $84\%$ of
wall-clock time has a model request outstanding, against ${\sim}5$
CPU-seconds of local computation --- so attrition's costs (correctness,
tokens) barely touch the metric teams watch. Capacity looks fine throughout: a
harness at $6.6\%$ utilisation gives no signal that it is discarding
evidence. And the common remedy masks the symptom: in this system a structured
prior procedure reached the same verdict as free exploration at $35\%$ of the
tokens, so teams that feel context pressure reach for structure and see
improvement --- the right fix for the wrong reason, needing less delivered
evidence rather than delivering it better.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\textbf{One harness, one domain, one model.} We argue the defect \emph{class}
generalises because the pipeline is unavoidable and because positional
bounding is what current frameworks ship (Sec.~\ref{sec:measured}); we have
not measured other harnesses end to end. \textbf{We authored the defect} ---
hence ablation with registered predictions rather than a repair narrative.
\textbf{Scale and bound}: $5$ recordings per class with paired repeats;
the ablation's bound is calibrated to its smaller traces (the mechanism and
the early/late delivery ratio are held constant; the production-scale replay
uses the deployed bound unmodified). \textbf{The accuracy-destruction half is
field evidence}, as detailed in Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}.

\section{Future Plans}

\textbf{(1) The causal accuracy experiment}, on a fault family with measured
repaired-arm headroom and a resident culprit, manipulating serialization order
with per-condition manipulation checks: culprit records visible, payload
validity, independent recoverability, and final diagnosis. \textbf{(2) A
bounding-code survey at depth}: extend Sec.~\ref{sec:measured}'s reading of
seven frameworks across the eleven-system corpus of~\cite{harness2026},
classifying positional versus structural bounds and testing each against the
delivery-cliff replay. \textbf{(3) Attrition profiling as a reporting
standard}: package the instrument so a harness emits an attrition profile
beside its accuracy number; the claim to test is that equal-accuracy harnesses
differ in evidence yield and that yield predicts transfer to harder task
distributions. \textbf{(4) Conformance tests}: the failures in
Table~\ref{tab:pipeline} are mechanical, so a harness can test for them in CI
without a model --- a result that does not survive its own bound, a step
budget with a spike at its limit, a phase boundary that drops computed
evidence. A replication package (drivers, scorer, per-cell results, replay
corpus, and the registered design with its dated amendment log) is prepared
for artifact submission.

\section{Conclusion}

An agent's context is not a window it is given but the output of a pipeline
its harness built. In a working system that pipeline destroyed a third of all
tool results while the window sat $93\%$ empty, deleted evidence by position
rather than value, and --- where redundancy allowed recovery --- converted the
loss into measurable extra work. Every harness makes these decisions; none
reports them. They are measurable, and we have shown how.
