Yes. I would actually make one important change to your idea.

I would not say we "collect intents over time." Intents are runtime requests. What you really collect and curate over time is observability knowledge, which is packaged as reusable Observability Blueprints.

Think of it like this:

Jira Issues / Incidents → examples of real problems
Designers / Observability experts → analyze many similar problems
Knowledge Engineering → create reusable blueprints
Runtime → automatically maps a new intent to one or more blueprints

So the architecture has an offline knowledge engineering phase and an online execution phase.

The key idea is that an Observability Blueprint is produced from repeated diagnostic knowledge, rather than directly from one Jira issue.

One Jira issue may contribute evidence for creating or improving a blueprint, but the blueprint represents the generalized procedure applicable to a class of problems.

A concise formulation is:

Many Historical Problems
        ↓
Generalized Diagnostic Knowledge
        ↓
Reusable Observability Blueprint
        ↓
Applied to New Intents
        ↓
Concrete Execution and Diagnosis
        ↓
Feedback Improves the Blueprint

This figure communicates a stronger message:

Historical problems are individual incidents.
Problem taxonomy organizes them into reusable categories.
Observability blueprints capture the expert diagnostic knowledge for an entire category, rather than a single incident.

That is much closer to how an experienced observability engineer thinks and how your architecture would likely evolve in practice.
